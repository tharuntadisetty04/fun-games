Why switching wins 2/3 of the time

When you first pick a door, you have a 1 in 3 chance of being right. The host — who always knows where the car is — opens a goat door. This doesn't change your original odds. Your door still carries a 1/3 probability.


But now the remaining 2/3 probability has been consolidated onto the single other door. Switching wins twice as often as staying. It seems wrong — but the math is clear, and the simulation proves it.
